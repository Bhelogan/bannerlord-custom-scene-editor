Custom Scene Creator

Build scenes for Mount & Blade II: Bannerlord from inside the game.
For Bannerlord v1.4.7

Open any scene the game ships, place anything the game ships, and take the result away as XML you can use in your own mod. No Modding Kit, no separate editor, no rebuild between changes.

1What this is

Bannerlord's scenes are built with the official Modding Kit — a separate multi-gigabyte download running a full editor outside the game. It is powerful and it is still the only way to do some things (terrain sculpting and unusual manual navmesh repairs among them). It is also a lot of machinery to start up before you can place a single crate.

This mod does the layout half in the game itself. You stand in the real scene, with the real lighting and the real ground under you, and put objects where they look right. What comes out the other end is plain XML.

What you can make with it

  • A reusable object — a fortified camp, a market stall, a pig pen. Exports as one prefab you can place anywhere, over and over.
  • A dressed scene — a graveyard laid out over an existing battle map, with spawn points for an ambush. Exports as a block you paste into a scene file.
  • A course — a race track marked with a start, numbered gates and a finish, for your own mod's code to read.

What it does not do

It does not sculpt terrain. Navmesh editing is authoring plus a safe file-side bake: CSC can cut walkable ground from beneath solid objects and add ground-level or elevated routes for tunnels, stairs, ramps, bridges, platforms, and wall walks. It does not replace every free-form repair in the official Modding Kit. See Limits.

2Installing

  1. Install and enable Bannerlord.Harmony. CSC uses it to keep all combat and character-action bindings out of its editing modes.
  2. Copy the CustomSceneCreator folder into your game's Modules folder.
  3. Enable Custom Scene Creator in the launcher's Singleplayer mod list.
  4. Optional: install Mod Configuration Menu (MCM) if you want to rebind keys. Everything works without it.
  5. Load a campaign — any campaign, including one you have been playing for two hundred hours.
The mod saves nothing into your campaign save. Your work lives in its own files under Documents, so you can uninstall the mod without touching your game, and your scenes survive a reinstall.

3Opening the editor

Walk into any town, village or castle and take the Open Scene Creator option on the settlement menu. That opens the project browser: saved work on the left, and New — Pick a Scene one button away.

If you have the developer console enabled, these do the same thing more directly:

CommandWhat it does

csc.open <scene>Open a specific scene by name, e.g. csc.open aserai_village_b
csc.projectsList your saved projects
csc.project <name>Reopen a saved project

The scene browser lists 611 scenes — battle terrain, multiplayer maps, towns, castles, villages, hideouts, arenas, interiors and naval — grouped and searchable.

Testing a saved scene

Select a saved project first. Open is for building. Walk Around loads that saved project as a normal non-editing mission, restores its placed objects, and spawns every healthy companion hero currently in your party plus five additional Empire peasants. All followers are on foot and follow the player. Use this mode to check collision, tunnels, narrow routes, stairs, ramps, bridges, platforms, and transitions between ground and elevated navmesh. Raid-Scale Battle restores the same saved layout and baked navmesh as a combat mission. Your healthy party members spawn beside you, while a matching raid-style force of bandits and looters spawns across the authored obstacle cluster. The test is capped at 100 agents per side and uses no horses. Neither mode writes casualties, temporary peasants, or roster changes back into the campaign.

Which test should I use? Walk Around is the precise traversal test: lead the followers through each doorway and tunnel, then up every stair, ramp, bridge, and wall walk. Raid-Scale Battle is the crowd and combat-routing test: watch whether formations route around solid cutouts and reach one another without piling up. When a project contains cutouts, CSC puts the two battle sides on opposite sides of that cutout cluster and at least 200 m apart. Projects without cutouts use a shorter fallback placement around the project, so add the required solid cutouts before treating the battle as an obstacle-routing proof.

The new-scene picker only selects a base map for editing. Testing is intentionally available only from Saved Projects, so there is always a placed layout and—when authored—a matching baked navmesh. Neither test option opens the editor or changes your campaign party; leaving returns to Saved Projects.

Saved Projects also has Bake Navmesh. It runs the project bake immediately, writes the result into the project’s Documents export folder, and reports progress/result in the browser and trace log. Use it before testing when you want an explicit bake step or need its refusal message.

When a saved project has navmesh work, CSC publishes its current baked mesh into one reusable test slot before opening Walk Around or Raid-Scale Battle. The slot is a disposable copy; your project and its durable bake remain in Documents. After installing the CSC version that adds this feature, restart Bannerlord once so it can register that one slot. After that first restart, changing scenes or bakes does not require another restart.

Scene levels

Many settlement scenes ship in several upgrade levels: the same village as a hamlet, a town, and a fortified town. The browser shows which levels a scene has and lets you pick. Level 1 is usually the sparsest and the easiest to work in.

Around 45 scenes are marked unopenable and hidden from the browser. These are campaign-map and atmosphere scenes that crash the game outright when loaded as a mission — the world map among them. Not a bug in your setup; they simply are not missions.

Leaving the editor (hold Tab) brings you back to the browser you came from, so trying several scenes in a row costs nothing. Close the browser to return to the settlement.

4Your first five minutes

Nothing here is permanent. Pick a battle terrain scene and try this:

  1. Press \. You switch to an overhead camera and enter Build mode.
  2. Press ` to open the asset picker. Type barrel in the search box, click a result, then Build.
  3. Move the mouse. A ghost of the barrel follows your cursor along the ground. Left-click to place it.
  4. Place a few more. Hold right mouse and move the mouse to tilt one; Q and E spin it; Left Ctrl resets it.
  5. Press \ twice more to reach Delete mode, and click one to remove it.
  6. Press L. Everything you placed is listed, with its exact position, rotation, and scale.
  7. Press Alt+S to save.

That is the whole loop. Everything after this is detail.

5Cameras

A scene opens in third person, walking around like any other mission — useful for judging how a place feels at eye level. Turning on an edit mode switches you to the overhead (RTS) camera, which is where most building happens. V cycles.

V also works while editing is Off. RTS is a free camera in every editor state: W A S D movement preserves the camera's absolute world height instead of following hills, valleys, indoor floors, or roofs. Use Space or Left Alt when you deliberately want to raise or lower it. Shift-flight can still move along the full viewing direction.

CameraPlacingGood for
OverheadWhere the cursor pointsLaying things out, seeing the whole site
Third personWhere you lookJudging scale against your character
First personWhere you lookDetail work; seeing exactly what a player will

Overhead camera

  • W A S D pan. Speed scales with height, so it does not crawl when you are up high.
  • Space and Left Alt raise and lower.
  • Hold Shift and drag to rotate the view; Shift+WASD flies along the view direction.

Once you have picked a camera with V, the editor stops changing it for you.

If you do not want the held asset to follow where your character looks, press V for the overhead camera. It is the free-building camera: the preview follows its independent cursor, while you pan, fly, and rotate the view separately.
There is no maximum placement range. If you can see it, you can build on it — from any height.
Wall-mounted objects: aim the preview at the wall, roof, floor, or other physical surface and press N. The editor samples the surface around the cursor and makes the object's flat face sit flush against it. Wall-mounted objects remain upright; use Q/E afterward to turn them within the surface. This is much more exact than trying to produce a 90-degree tilt by eye.

6Edit modes

The row across the top of the Assets build selector selects Build, Delete, Move, Scripts, and the navmesh tools directly, then returns you to the editor. \ still cycles through all nine modes (including Off) if you prefer the keyboard. The selected mode decides what a left-click does.

ModeLeft-click
OffNothing. You are just walking around.
BuildPlaces the current object
DeleteRemoves what you click
MovePicks up what you click; click again to put it down
ScriptOpens the scripts attached to what you click
NavmeshSets route point A, then B; a third click starts over
Navmesh Cutout PlanRecords/removes a padded footprint that becomes a hole when you save
Add Navmesh AreaMarks/removes ground that should become walkable when you save
Elevated NavmeshAuthors a raised walkable strip for stairs, ramps, bridges, and wall walks
Navmesh Cutout Plan mode marks what should stop being walkable. Click a solid object placed by this editor to measure its physics footprint, add 0.75 m clearance, and draw the perimeter. The footprint is saved with the project.
Add Navmesh Area marks the opposite problem. Click unmeshed terrain where agents will need to walk to mark a 4 m area. Aim near its center and click again to remove it. The mark samples the terrain height around its rim, so the ground built there follows slopes and steps instead of lying flat across them. Overlapping marks are filled as one continuous surface.
Elevated Navmesh is for surfaces above the terrain. Trace four or more physical corners of one stair, ramp, bridge deck, or wall-walk in order around its perimeter, then press F to close it. There are no left/right rails to manage. A cyan cross and dot follows your aim and shows the exact physical surface hit where the next corner will land—use it when working inside a building in first or third person. The corners retain the height you clicked on; they are not pulled down to terrain. Click a saved dot to select it, click again to move it, or press Delete to remove it. A draft remains visible and is saved with the project when you change modes. After closing one area, left-click to begin another. Four corners are the minimum, but add as many perimeter corners as the physical shape needs. Only closed areas are included in a bake. While an area is open, only its own dots can be selected; a new area may safely share an exact corner with a completed ramp or platform.
Making a reusable stair, bridge, or defensive wall: author its elevated outline before choosing Export as Prefab. The exported prefab carries that outline in its own local coordinates, alongside its visible parts. When the prefab is later used by Homesteads, the homestead bake moves and rotates the walkable route with the placed asset—the player does not draw navmesh. Copy the finished XML into the Homesteads Prefabs folder under a unique hsr_ name, then add that same prefab name to the Homesteads placeables list. Keep the outline on the actual walking surface; add a cutout only where ground below the asset must be blocked, not beneath an ordinary raised bridge deck.

Navmesh baking

The navmesh is the invisible surface that tells everyone in the scene where they can walk. Placing a house does not change it by itself: without a bake, soldiers walk straight through the walls, and ground you cleared stays off-limits. Baking is what turns your three kinds of marks — footprints that should become holes, ground that should become walkable, and completed elevated outlines — into an actual navmesh.

You do not need external tools to bake. It happens with your save, or you can use Bake Navmesh from Saved Projects to create or refresh a test bake on demand:

  • When you save a project, the baked navmesh is written beside it, in Documents\Mount and Blade II Bannerlord\CustomSceneCreator\exports\navmesh\, named after the project. The save message tells you what it did — how many footprints were cut out, how many areas filled, and how many were skipped.
  • When you export a Modding Kit scene, the navmesh inside that exported scene folder is baked too. The folder you get is complete: scene, objects, and matching navmesh.

The game's own scene files are never written to. Baking only ever produces files in your own Documents folder, and it keeps the original as .prebake where it replaces anything.

A bake never fails halfway. Each footprint is applied on its own, and one that cannot be done cleanly — a shape that would split the walkable ground in two, or one that reaches off the edge of the mesh — is skipped and named in the message, while everything else still bakes.

Checking the whole map

Watching a few soldiers fight only tests the ground they fight on. To check the whole scene, open the console and run: 

csc.navmesh_audit <project name>

It walks the entire baked navmesh and reports, with coordinates you can walk to:

  • Cut-off ground — walkable areas nothing can reach from the rest of the map. This is the failure that is invisible until someone needs to make that journey.
  • Objects still walkable — a footprint you marked that is still navmesh, so agents will walk through that building.
  • Marked ground still unwalkable — an area you marked that did not get filled, so agents will refuse to go there.
  • Broken or overlapping geometry.

Every finding is also marked in the scene: a ring on the ground with a tall mast above it, red where movement will break and amber where something is merely worth a look. The marks stay visible in every edit mode so you can walk to one without also holding the right mode, and csc.navmesh_audit_clear hides them again.

Run this before you ship a scene. csc.navtest is still the way to watch agents actually move once the audit is clean.

Navmesh mode is read-only. The panel shows the cursor's face, group, island, and surface-to-navmesh height difference. Click A and B to test infantry reachability, path distance, detour ratio, and direct-line clearance. The points are temporary and are not saved or exported.
While an edit mode is on, all character combat and action controls are disabled in the third- and first-person cameras. Attacks, blocks, kicks, jumps, crouching, weapon draw/sheath, weapon slots, mounting, interaction, and remapped equivalents cannot animate the character. Walking and looking still work so you can reach interiors and inspect detail. Everything returns to normal as soon as editing is off.

Delete and Move only ever touch your own objects. The scene's original contents are not editable, so you cannot accidentally delete a village.

Rebinding

If you have Mod Configuration Menu (MCM) installed, every key above except the mouse buttons can be changed under Mod Options → Custom Scene Creator. Changes apply when you next open a scene — no restart. MCM is optional; without it the editor uses the defaults.

Key names are physical positions on a US keyboard, not the letter printed on the cap. On AZERTY, the key named Q is the one labelled A. Turn on Key Detection Mode in the settings, open a scene and press a key — the editor tells you the exact name to type. Turn it off when you are done.

A binding left blank falls back to its default rather than unbinding, so an empty box cannot leave part of the editor unreachable. A name that is not a real key is ignored, with an explanation in the log.

Ground follow

On by default: the object you are holding hugs the terrain as you move, which is what you want for fences, walls and props. H pins it at the current height instead — for a bridge, a balcony, or anything that should float. G drops it back down and turns following on again.

8Choosing what to build

` opens the asset picker: 5,838 objects across 19 categories, plus your own markers and prefabs.

  • Category — a dropdown; All is first.
  • Search — filters within the chosen category. Separate words may appear in any order: arabian interior house matches an asset containing all three words anywhere in its name or tags. Pick All first to search everything.
  • Info panel — what the selected object is: its meshes, whether it has collision, which module it comes from.
  • Build, or double-click, to take it.

Whatever the picker leaves you with is also what [ and ] cycle through — so after searching cart, those keys step through carts. ' leaves the search behind and moves to the next whole category.

Using your own mod's assets

The picker lists the base game's prefabs only. That is deliberate — a prefab from someone else's mod is a dead entry for anyone who does not have it — but it is not a limit. Rebuild the asset list with every module included and your own mod's prefabs appear in the picker, placeable exactly like the base game's: 

powershell -File tools\regenerate\build_asset_dump.ps1 -IncludeAllModules

Restart the game afterwards; your prefabs are grouped under your module's name. This is the intended way to lay out a scene from assets you are making yourself.

Keep that dump to yourself. It describes your installation, so anyone else would get entries for mods they do not have. See tools\regenerate\README_REGENERATE.md.
Some objects have no collision of their own — candle flames, torch flames, banners. They are still placeable and still selectable, but a player will walk straight through them. That is how the base game ships them; the flame is meant to sit on a torch that does have collision.

9Markers

What they are for

A marker is an object with no geometry — a labelled position. It shows in the editor as a plain cube or cylinder so you can see and click it, and exports as a named, tagged entity that code can find. This is how you tell your mod put the ambush here.

They live in the Editor Markers category:

Facing matters. A marker's rotation is exported with it, so a spawn point should be turned to face the way you want whatever appears there to look. Rotate it like any other object.

Adding your own

Markers are declared in data, not code. Drop an XML file into Modules/CustomSceneCreator/ModuleData/packs/ and it is picked up at load — no rebuild: 

<Placeables>
  <Placeable id="my_treasure_spot" display="Treasure Spot" category="My Markers"
             proxy="editor_cube" exportName="treasure_{index}" exportTag="treasure" />
</Placeables>

proxy is any real prefab to stand in for it visually — editor_cube, editor_cylinder, editor_sphere and editor_marker all ship with the game. {index} becomes the marker's number.

Write your own file rather than editing csc_core.xml, which a mod update will overwrite. If a pack file has an XML error, every marker in it is lost — the editor says so on screen when it loads, and the details go to the log.

Numbering

Numbered markers are numbered as you place them: eight enemy spawns give you sp_enemy_1 through sp_enemy_8 with no effort. The number belongs to the marker from the moment it exists, so it does not shuffle later.

New markers take the lowest free number for their type. Delete gate 3, place a replacement, and it is gate 3 again — not gate 9 at the end of the track.

To renumber by hand, select the marker in the scene contents list (L) and edit Marker #. It shows the name the marker will actually export under. Duplicates are allowed — two spawns numbered 2 is a fair way to say either of these — but you will be told, because the usual cause is a typo.

10Scripts

Scripts are what make a scene move: a fire burning, a windmill turning, a chair someone can sit in. Switch to Script mode with \ and click an object.

The panel shows what is attached, and lets you add from the 130 scripts the game ships — searchable, grouped, and ordered by how often the real scenes use each one, since that ordering is far more useful than alphabetical.

Editing variables

  • Bools are true/false buttons. The chosen one is lit and marked.
  • Numbers and text are typed, with examples from real scenes beside them.
  • Values used by shipped scenes — where a variable has known values, a Choose button lists them, searchable and ranked by how many scenes use each.
The Choose list matters most for things nobody can type from memory. A sound emitter's Event Path wants something like event:/mission/ambient/detail/river_01 — those names live inside the game's sound banks and are not written down anywhere you can read. The list is built by reading what the game's own scenes use.

Most scripts run live as soon as you attach them, so a fire lights immediately. A few cannot — siege equipment and deployment points need a siege mission to exist in. Those are flagged in the panel, and are still exported normally; they simply will not preview here.

Entity references — AnimationPoint.PairEntity, which links two posed NPCs — are shown read-only. Pairing needs a click-to-pick-target mode that does not exist yet. Set those in the Modding Kit, or with the bake script.

11Scene contents list

L lists everything you have placed. Clicking in the world only reaches what is in front of you; this reaches what is buried inside a building, behind you, or too small to put a cursor on.

  • Search by object name or by attached script.
  • Sort by nearest, name, newest, or which objects carry scripts.
  • Go To moves the camera to it without changing edit mode.
  • Pick Up, or double-clicking an item, closes the List and makes that object active in Move mode.
  • Scripts, Textures, and Delete act on the selected object.
  • Break Apart is available for a composite imported under My Prefabs. After confirmation, it replaces that one instance with the original child prefabs and restores embedded Cutout, Add Area, and Elevated Navmesh authoring in editable world coordinates. Child position, rotation, scale, and supported scripts are preserved. The reusable prefab file itself is not deleted.
  • Clear All starts over after confirmation: it removes every placed object and every authored navmesh cutout, added area, and elevated area. It never removes anything from the underlying base scene. Save the project afterward to keep the cleared state.
Break Apart restores the navmesh authoring that was embedded when the prefab was exported. New exports retain cutout ownership as well as added and elevated areas. Older prefab files can only restore the authoring fields they already contain, and their recovered cutouts are independent bakeable areas rather than child-owned cutouts. A texture override assigned to the whole composite still cannot be distributed among its children. The confirmation reports limitations before changing the scene. If any child cannot be created, if the composite contains anonymous geometry that has no reusable prefab identity, or if scripts are attached to the composite as a whole, the original prefab is left untouched.

Exact placement

Selecting an object shows its position in metres, rotation in degrees, and scale on its local X, Y, and Z axes as editable fields. Typed numbers are the only way to be exact: lining a wall up with the one beside it, or spacing race gates evenly, is arithmetic rather than aim.

Enter the same scale on X, Y, and Z for ordinary uniform resizing: 0.5, 0.5, 0.5 makes an object half size and 2, 2, 2 doubles it. Different values stretch individual local axes and are useful for simple barriers, floors, and blockout pieces, but can visibly distort a detailed asset. Values must be between 0.01 and 100. Scale is saved in the project, restored in both test modes, and written into prefab and scene exports.

Scaling the placed root also scales physics inherited by that root, but a prefab can contain unusual detached or scripted collision. Test collision after resizing. A marked navmesh cutout is refreshed automatically; hand-drawn Add Area or Elevated Navmesh outlines are world-space work, so redraw those outlines after changing the supporting object's scale.

Texture overrides

The contents list can replace one of a placed object's material images without changing every other copy of that prefab. Put an 8-bit, non-interlaced grayscale, RGB, or RGBA PNG in Documents\Mount and Blade II Bannerlord\CustomSceneCreator\textures\, press L, select the object, and choose Textures.

  1. Cycle through the object's exact material names.
  2. Choose the PNG and press Apply Preview.
  3. Use Refresh PNGs after editing the image; CSC rereads its pixels without requiring you to leave the scene.
  4. Save, then verify it in Walk Around and Raid-Scale Battle.
This replaces the material's visible diffuse image slots on this object instance. Some Bannerlord materials draw their colour from a secondary diffuse slot, so CSC updates that slot too when the source material uses it. It does not create UV coordinates, change the mesh's UV mapping, crop an atlas, or create a new shader. Transparent pixels work only when the original material already supports transparency. CSC preserves the original shader and alpha state for safety.

Projects and built-in tests apply the override automatically. Textured templates must be shared as both <name>.json and <name>_textures/; CSC imports the images when it previews or places that template.

Prefab and scene XML cannot embed a PNG created at runtime. Every textured export therefore adds <name>.texture_overrides.json and <name>_textures/. Another mod must ship and consume that sidecar, or convert the image into its own registered material resources. Copying only the XML preserves the object but loses the runtime texture. See MOD_INTEGRATION.md.
A quick way to space things evenly: place them roughly, then fix one coordinate at a time. Setting every gate to the same Y and stepping X by 20 gives a straight, even run in seconds.

12Saving and exporting

Projects are what you reopen

Alt+S saves your project — everything placed, plus which scene and which levels it belongs to. Reopening restores the whole session rather than dropping objects into whatever scene happened to be open. Leaving with unsaved changes asks first.

A reminder sits in the top right whenever there is unsaved work, with the object count and the save keys. It stays up in every mode, including with editing switched off — the exit prompt catches the obvious way to lose work, but not a crash or an alt-tab-and-forget.

FolderContents
projects/Your working files. This is what you reopen.
textures/PNG working library used by per-object material overrides
exports/prefabs/Prefab XML — one reusable object, including local cutout/elevated metadata you authored
exports/scenes/Absolute-coordinate scene fragments — used by scene-specific layouts such as racetracks
exports/navmesh-cutouts/Readable navmesh plans (.navcut.json); Bannerlord does not load these directly
exports/navmesh/Durable baked navmesh for each saved project
Modules/CustomSceneCreator/SceneObj/csc_*/Complete derived scenes for the Modding Kit
exports/templates/Templates — a layout to place into other scenes and adapt

All under Documents\Mount and Blade II Bannerlord\CustomSceneCreator\.

A project is the source; an export is the finished artifact. To keep working on something, reopen the project — not the prefab you exported from it.

Templates — reuse something you can still edit

A prefab is placed whole, which is right for something you reuse unchanged. A CSC composite imported under My Prefabs can later be selected in the scene contents list and Broken Apart into the addressable child prefabs stored inside it. This is useful for occasional revisions. Its embedded Cutout, Add Area, and Elevated Navmesh authoring is restored with the children; a whole-prefab texture override is not distributed to them.

When you already know a layout is meant to stay editable, Alt+E offers a third choice: Export as Template. It writes to exports/templates/ and appears in the asset picker under My Templates. Place one and its contents drop into the scene as individual objects — each one selectable, movable, deletable, exactly as if you had placed them by hand. Rotate before placing and the whole layout turns with you.

The preview shows the whole template, not a marker standing in for it — a pole at the centre tells you nothing about whether a hundred-piece fort will clear the trees or hang off a slope, which is the only question worth asking before you drop it.

Templates need no restart. A prefab has to be read by the game at startup; a template is a file this mod reads itself, so it is placeable the moment you export it — the picker updates as soon as the export dialog closes.
Templates contain the visible, separately editable objects only. They do not carry the project's cutout, ground-addition, or elevated-navmesh authoring into the destination project. Redraw those marks after placing the template, or use a prefab/complete-scene export when navigation must travel with the artifact.

This is the way to build the same thing across several maps: lay it out once, export it as a template, then open each map and place it where it suits that ground. A template without texture overrides is a single file you can send to someone. A textured template also needs its sibling <name>_textures/ directory; keep the two together under exports/templates/.

13Using what you made

A prefab

Prefab exports are written to your Documents folder and copied into Modules/CustomSceneCreator/Prefabs/. Restart the game — prefab XML is only read at startup — and your object appears in the asset picker under My Prefabs, placeable as a single piece.

The picker lists whatever is in exports/prefabs, and that folder is also the archive — it survives a reinstall and it is what you send to someone else. Anything you drop in there, a prefab from another machine or another modder, is copied into the module's Prefabs folder the next time the editor opens, so it is loadable however the game resolves it. Restart once and it places.

To ship it in your own mod, copy the XML into your module's Prefabs folder. Then anywhere in code: 

GameEntity.Instantiate(mission.Scene, "my_fortified_camp", frame);
Prefab XML carries cutout and elevated-route authoring metadata, but Bannerlord does not apply that metadata by itself. If your mod places the prefab dynamically, it must use a compatible file-side baker before the target scene loads. Instantiating the prefab creates its visible parts; it does not change the stock scene's navmesh. Homesteads Reloaded is an example of a consuming mod that performs this bake.

A scene

Choose Create Modding Kit Scene. The editor clones the base scene, renames it, and inserts everything you placed into a complete workbench scene at: 

Modules/CustomSceneCreator/SceneObj/csc_<name>/
  1. Close the retail game and start the Modding Kit with CustomSceneCreator enabled.
  2. Open the new csc_* scene. Read CSC_MODDING_KIT_HANDOFF.txt in its folder if you need the source and selected-level details.
  3. Only if you need the Kit's own navmesh work: the export already carries a baked navmesh for every footprint you marked, so re-bake here only for changes the editor could not express, then use File → Save Navigation Mesh. When only navigation changed, avoid the broader Save Scene operation; current Kit builds may crash while staging terrain.
  4. Reopen it in our editor with csc.open csc_<name> and repeat the Navmesh A/B tests.
  5. Move the finished SceneObj folder into the module that will own the scene before release. Move matching SceneEditData only if you want future Modding Kit editing; the retail game does not need it.
The exporter bakes the folder's navmesh for every footprint and area you marked, so the scene leaves here complete. Anything the bake had to skip is named in the export message — check it, and run csc.navmesh_audit before you ship. Objects you never marked do not change walkable ground by themselves. Existing csc_* folders are never overwritten, because they may contain hours of Kit work.

A baked navmesh.bin belongs to one exact scene and terrain. Do not copy it onto another map. The csc_navmesh_test_slot_* folders are disposable Walk Around/Battle test caches, not release content. The release also includes MOD_INTEGRATION.md as a concise checklist for mod authors.

From mod code

Markers are how a scene talks to your mod. They export with tags, and tags are what you search for: 

// Every enemy spawn you placed, in the order they are found
List<GameEntity> spawns = mission.Scene.FindEntitiesWithTag("sp_enemy").ToList();

foreach (GameEntity spawn in spawns) {
    MatrixFrame frame = spawn.GetGlobalFrame();
    // frame.origin is the position, frame.rotation the facing you set in the editor
}

// Or a specific one, by the number the editor gave it
GameEntity gate3 = mission.Scene.FindEntityWithName("race_gate_3");

Search by tag when you want all of a kind — every enemy spawn, every patrol point. Search by name when the number matters — gate 3 comes after gate 2.

If you want your own tag names rather than sp_enemy, do not rename markers by hand. The bake script's retag stage maps them all in one pass.

14The bake script

tools/bake_scene.py post-processes an export. You may not need it — what the editor writes already works. Baking is for what comes after layout, done the same way every time. 

python bake_scene.py --init-config      # write a documented config
python bake_scene.py my_graveyard.xml   # bake it
python bake_scene.py my_graveyard.xml --list   # just show what is in the file
StageWhat it does
markersTurns a bare marker into a working entity — an NPC work spot needs a UsablePlace with an AnimationPoint under it
scriptsAttaches a script to everything matching a rule — every torch burns
retagRenames tags to whatever your mod looks for
physicsAdds collision shapes to mesh-based entities
guidsGives every entity an ID, so scripts can reference each other
deployCopies the result where it has to live

Everything is driven by bake_config.json, and the script is meant to be edited. Full documentation is in tools/README_BAKE.md.

The physics stage usually reports doing nothing to an editor export, which is correct: objects you place are prefab references, and a prefab carries its own collision. That stage is for files whose geometry is spelled out mesh by mesh.

15Worked example: a graveyard ambush

A scene where the player walks into a graveyard and is jumped. Start to finish:

  1. Pick the ground. Open the scene browser and choose a battle terrain map with the right feel. Nothing you do will alter the original.
  2. Dress it. \ into Build mode, ` for the picker, and search grave, fence, ruin. Place headstones; a fence to shape the space; a gate where you want the player to enter.
  3. Light it. Place braziers, then \ to Script mode, click one, and attach LightCycle. It lights immediately, so you can judge it in place.
  4. Mark the entrance. Category Editor Markers → Player Spawn. Face it toward the graveyard — the rotation is exported.
  5. Place the ambush. Enemy Spawns behind the headstones, facing in. They are numbered 1, 2, 3… as you go. Use the second wave's numbers deliberately if you want one: 1–4 ahead, 5–8 behind.
  6. Check the layout. L, sort by name, and confirm the spawns read 1 through 8 with none missing. Nudge positions with the number fields if any are inside a wall.
  7. Save with Alt+S, then Alt+E and choose Create Modding Kit Scene — this belongs to one particular map.
  8. Open the generated scene. It is already cloned, renamed, and populated under CustomSceneCreator/SceneObj/csc_*; no XML pasting is needed.
  9. Check the CSC bake result. Resolve every skipped operation, then use Walk Around or Raid-Scale Battle. Open the Modding Kit only if you need a manual navmesh edit CSC cannot express.
  10. Spawn them from your mission code:
foreach (GameEntity spawn in mission.Scene.FindEntitiesWithTag("sp_enemy")) {
    MatrixFrame frame = spawn.GetGlobalFrame();
    mission.SpawnAgent(new AgentBuildData(character)
        .InitialPosition(frame.origin)
        .InitialDirection(frame.rotation.f.AsVec2));
}

Steps 1 to 7 are the part that used to need the Modding Kit, and they take about twenty minutes.

16Limits

Worth knowing before you plan something around them.

LimitWhat it means
No terrain editingYou cannot raise a hill or dig a ditch. Terrain lives in a binary file the game does not expose. Pick a scene whose ground already suits you, or shape space with objects.
Navmesh baking is file-sideSaving bakes the navmesh into a file, but the mission you are standing in keeps the one it loaded with. To see a bake take effect, load the exported scene.
No undoDelete mode and the contents list are the ways back. Save often.
Scaling is root-localThe contents list can resize a placed prefab on X, Y, and Z. Complex scripted prefabs may contain collision or interaction points that were designed for their original size, so verify them in Walk Around and Raid-Scale Battle.
A moment on leavingThe browser reappears a frame or two after the mission closes, since it has to attach to the screen underneath once that screen exists.
Entity references read-onlyScripts pointing at another entity cannot be linked here yet.
Original scene contents are fixedYou can add to a scene but not remove or move what shipped in it.
Texture overrides reuse existing UVs and shadersCSC can replace a material's diffuse PNG per object, but it cannot invent UV mapping or safely turn an opaque material into a transparent shader. Exports need their texture manifest and image folder.

17Troubleshooting

The Editor Markers category is missing

A marker pack failed to load — the editor says so on screen when the scene opens. The cause is almost always an XML error in a file under ModuleData/packs/. The log names the file and the line. Note a run of dashes inside an XML comment is illegal, which is an easy trap.

My exported prefab is not in the picker

Restart the game. Prefab XML is read only at startup. Until then it is listed as needing a restart rather than hidden.

My exported prefab has no ghost and will not place

Re-export it. Prefabs exported before this was fixed referenced their parts by name, and a world prefab cannot do that — only a scene can. Such a file loads without error and draws nothing, which is why there is no preview to place. Exports now copy each part's real definition into the file.

NPCs walk through my walls, or will not enter

Open the saved project and use Rebake Navmesh. Read the full result: a skipped cutout leaves a wall walkable, while a skipped or disconnected elevated outline prevents entry or stops followers partway up. Confirm solid objects were marked with Cutout, stairs/ramps/tunnels were closed in Elevated Navmesh, then use Walk Around to test actual agents. Remember that the editor mission keeps the mesh it loaded with; the new bake appears in the test mission or complete exported scene, not live under your feet.

The game closed itself while I was browsing assets

A few prefabs carry scripts written for a specific mission, and instantiating one outside that mission can fault inside the engine. That is not something a mod can catch — the game exits, with no crash report to send.

The editor handles this in two ways. Most such scripts are removed before they can run, so the preview is a plain, still object. If one gets through anyway, the name of whatever was being built is written down first — so on your next visit that prefab is blocked, you are told which one it was, and everything else keeps working. You lose one session per bad prefab, once.

The list lives beside the log, in csc_unsafe_prefabs.txt. Delete a line to try that prefab again. It is worth sending on — it is exactly what the shipped guard needs to improve.

I cannot select a small object

Get closer, or use the contents list (L) — it reaches anything regardless of size or what is in front of it.

Left-click swings my weapon instead of placing

You are not in an edit mode. Press \.

The cursor is locked to the middle of the screen

Alt-tabbing away and back can do this. Alt-tab once more, or press V twice.

A scene crashes when I open it

Some scenes are not missions and cannot be loaded as one; the known ones are hidden from the browser. If you found another by name with csc.open, that is why.
